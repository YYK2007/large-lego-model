# LEGO Builder visual review and Sprout changes

Reviewed 2026-10-10. I opened the official App Store carousel and played, paused, and advanced the 37-second explainer on LEGO's site in Chrome, inspecting screenshots of the rendered pages. LEGO Builder was absent from the available native-app inventory. **This was inspection of official published screens and video, not operation of the native Builder app or a completed physical build.** The proposals below are our design decisions, not claims about undocumented Builder behavior.

## Evidence actually viewed

The [official Builder page](https://www.lego.com/en-us/builder-app) shows a landscape instruction canvas with a narrow left tray: rendered pieces labeled `x2`/`x1`, a bag badge, and large lower-corner back/next buttons. Pale, translucent-looking pieces contrast with an otherwise colored vehicle. In its “Watch now” explainer, 00:07 shows distinct Start Building and Build Together actions; 00:11 shows a rotatable model and reset icon; 00:22 shows two avatars, a module handoff and Done; 00:26 shows a green part outline, a progress indicator, and a resume card. These are separate captured moments; the reviewed footage did not establish a full per-piece insertion animation or a universal ghosting rule.

The [official App Store screenshots](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lego-builder-3d-instructions/id1486159728) show theme/search cards, a zoom/rotation illustration, and a Build Together lobby with participant avatars, a party number and a waiting state. Its developer description confirms PIN-based joining, divided tasks and saved progress. A pictured builder count belongs to that example, not a verified universal session limit.

LEGO's [getting-started help](https://www.lego.com/en-us/service/help-topics/article/lego-builder-app-3d-building-instructions) makes the set-dependent distinction between 3D, PDF, Build Together and Missions. A PDF-only instruction set is not evidence of interactive 3D support. For deeper collaboration context, a [first-hand UX researcher case study](https://www.linestampe.com/projects/legobuilder) describes testing version 3.1.5, independently built modules, handovers and recovery problems. It is historical research, not current official app documentation.

## Immediate changes for our existing React/Three viewer

The main gap is guidance about the next physical action. A camera, layer slider and generic “add this layer” sentence do not tell someone which piece to pick up or where to put it. Keep a large clean model canvas and devote the surrounding controls to that action.

1. **Gather tray.** Derive the current step's quantities from actual instances, grouped by exact part and color. Show an isometric piece thumbnail, quantity, color name and dimensions. Put the part number in secondary text. Selecting a tray item highlights only its placements and shows their coordinates. The sum of tray quantities must equal the newly added instance count, including identical copies.
2. **Place, then confirm.** For the present upright brick catalog, provide a deterministic per-brick sequence within each layer. Keep completed geometry visible, give the current brick a clear outline and target marker, and show its final orientation. Use a short vertical approach animation only when that insertion path is valid. Add Replay and a static placement view; honor reduced motion. An animation is explanatory and must never change the canonical saved transform or imply a successful physical placement.
3. **Useful highlighting.** Retain recognizable assembly color and silhouette. Prefer a current-part outline plus a modest desaturation of earlier parts over making the whole assembly nearly invisible. Use a “show hidden placement” action for occluded targets; label any temporary cutaway or ghost mode. Do not confuse faded material with a missing brick, especially for white/transparent parts. Show a textual cue as well as color.
4. **Camera continuity.** Preserve the user's orbit when advancing. Offer Reset step view, Top and Front, with an orientation marker shared by the placement map. A deliberate rotation instruction should be shown before moving the camera. Pause auto-rotation while building. Do not force an unrelated view on every Next press.
5. **Physical progress.** Label previous/next consistently and make them large enough for touch. Show step and piece progress separately. Persist confirmed progress against the immutable design revision; an edited model starts or explicitly migrates a session instead of inheriting an unrelated step index. Back and Replay must be safe, including when an animation is active. Reaching the final screen is distinct from the user confirming the real build is finished.
6. **Keep help near the action.** Include the current part's footprint, orientation and precise top-down map in the web step view, not exclusively behind the PDF download. Describe placement relative to a visible neighbor where it can be derived accurately. Coordinate details remain available for resolving ambiguity.

For implementation, `BrickScene` can accept the active instance ID, committed visible IDs and a replay token in addition to layer highlights. Keep display offsets on a transient wrapper group. A small deterministic step adapter can emit `{instanceIds, activeInstanceId, completedIds}` from the shared design without asking the language model to invent counts. Reuse the shared geometry factory for thumbnails. Do not create a second parts list or transform convention inside the UI.

## Subassemblies and multiplayer

First add real assembly hierarchy and validated join operations as described in [complex-builds.md](complex-builds.md). A module view needs a local camera, its own gather tray, a clear `build 2` instruction when repeated, and a parent joining view that shows matching faces and insertion direction. Global piece counts include every copy even if instructions are reused.

A future collaborative session should assign independent modules, reserve their exact pieces, show who owns each task, and explicitly request the transfer when another builder needs it. A receiving builder confirms the handoff. Allow a participant to recover, pause or reassign unfinished work without losing everyone's progress. Synchronizing one global step counter across devices is not equivalent to splitting a build. These changes require real session state; a room code alone should not be presented as working multiplayer.

## Manual appearance: stronger Frutiger Aero, with precise diagrams

The requested direction is Windows XP-led Frutiger Aero/Y2K: rounded blue window frames, sky and grass greens, and glossy controls. The current PDF already has generous white space, small gradients and blue/green accents, but the repeated thin header strip reads closer to a technical report. Concentrate the stronger framing on the cover and navigation, leaving assembly geometry easy to inspect.

- Give the cover a luminous sky-blue gradient, a grass-green curved horizon and one rounded blue window frame with a glossy title bar and fine white highlight. Place the finished model large on its pale inner panel with a restrained contact shadow. Keep the title on an opaque or near-opaque field so contrast stays reliable. Decorative window controls must look like printed framing, not actionable PDF buttons.
- Use rounded, softly shaded blue step-number capsules and a pale glass-like gather tray. Repeat the same icon positions and accent palette in the web viewer. Avoid large ornamental bubbles behind studs or coordinates.
- Keep step backgrounds near white, old/new-part contrast clear, outlines crisp and orientation cues fixed. Use an illustrated quantity tray above the main assembly image and an unobtrusive secondary placement map below it. Do not let a decorative gradient change how a part color is perceived.
- Keep exact IDs, source-set allocations, shortages and revision identifiers readable. A polished cover cannot substitute for complete instructions or honest structural limitations.

Before claiming the redesign is finished, inspect the cover, a crowded layer, white/black pieces, a partly hidden placement, a repeated-module join and the final BOM at full size and print scale. Web acceptance should include keyboard navigation, a narrow phone viewport, reduced motion, reload/resume, replay during navigation, and exact tray-to-step counts. Test renderer occlusion first: a false gap in a solid model undermines every instruction treatment.
