# Large Lego Model: verification record

This document distinguishes tested local behavior from live integrations that need account/device access.

## Automated checks

The current suite has **138 passing tests**. `npm test` covers malformed placements and metadata, exact shape/color preservation through packing, interlocked seams, collision/support/connectivity gates, BOM/step consistency, inventory quantities and provenance, CSV escaping, LDraw units/orientation, PDF placement maps and page overflow, immutable saved revisions, and Photon message parsing/deduplication/session handling. New coverage includes uncapped piece counts, explicit budgets, bulk inventory, V2/legacy normalization, plate heights, four slope orientations and partial stud coverage, repeated-feature plans, exact web/native geometry, streaming progress, provider deadlines/errors, and the detailed castle's openings and placement order.

`npm run build` passes its strict TypeScript check and production build. Vite still reports a bundle-size advisory for the Three.js-containing main chunk (about 970 KB minified, 243 KB gzip). At checkpoint 02, `npm audit` was reduced from 18 moderate transitive findings to zero by pinning `@opentelemetry/core` to 2.12.0; no dependency change or fresh audit was part of checkpoint 03.

## Checkpoint 03: uncapped, more complex builds

- The UI no longer has a piece slider or sends a default budget. Generation, compiler and model validation have no application piece-count ceiling. Exact owned-only stock and explicitly supplied positive-safe-integer budgets still apply. Occupied-cell and shape-primitive caps were removed. Physical extent remains 24 × 24 studs × 72 plate heights; payload/provider/deadline limits are separate.
- The planner can emit boxes, real 3039 slope runs, and compact translated groups for repeated architecture. Instructions ask for actual hollow structures, multiple elevations, openings, floors, roofs and connected details. Geometry checks do not establish semantic faithfulness to every request.
- The curated **Courtyard castle** (`sample-castle`) contains **1,345 pieces**, 19 furnished rooms, four hollow four-storey towers, a three-storey keep, a front gate, courtyard lanes, open windows and five pitched roofs (72 slope pieces). At checkpoint 03 its frozen placements exactly matched recompiling `shared/complex-plan.ts`. The additive narrow-plate palette later repacks the same colored occupied space into 1,369 pieces; the saved 1,345-piece sample and its existing exports remain unchanged. Regression checks verify room floors/table pieces, window/gate/courtyard voids, exact BOM quantities, and every elevated placement having an earlier supporting placement. Towers and keep do not have full stairs/circulation; do not claim they do.
- Independent full generation admission of that curated plan took **39 ms**, with geometry validation around **6 ms**, on this Mac. This proves the local pipeline can handle intricate geometry; it is not evidence of AI generation quality. A separate 2,352-piece connected fixture passes the spatial validator and still detects an introduced overlap.
- Inspected the castle from four azimuths and a first-floor cutaway. In the actual browser, reviewed the complete model and step 61, advanced from piece 1 to 2 with matching part/color/coordinate changes, and verified restored step/piece progress. At 390-pixel phone width, fixed the tall model overlapping its title by reserving canvas space below the heading; document and body widths both remain 390 pixels. Each step contains at most 12 pieces; multiple steps can share a height. Progress storage was versioned to avoid interpreting old layer indices as new step indices.
- Web instancing shares geometry and materials. A separate validated **1,038-piece rendering specimen** used **10 draw calls/frame**, averaging **8.30 ms** between rendered frames (p95 **10.3 ms**, 603 frames in five seconds) on an Apple M5 Pro / ANGLE Metal at 1440 × 1000. A stationary five-second sample made no draw calls. These are machine-specific rendering measurements, not generation speed or quality results.
- The castle produces **127 build steps**, a **131-page PDF** (22.8 MB; 19.6 seconds to generate), and a **2.7 MB USDZ** (48 ms). PDF cover, BOM/guide, middle and final-step pages were rendered and inspected; all step headers, placed counts, coordinates and footer numbers were checked from extracted text. USDZ archive integrity passes. Exact BOM total is 1,345. At checkpoint 03, this work ran on the API thread; checkpoint 04 moves it to a worker, as recorded below.
- Local review evidence: `output/complex-local/` (four angles, cutaway, browser preview/assembly, benchmark, native export), `output/pdf/courtyard-castle-instructions.pdf`, `output/pdf/castle-pages/`, and `output/checkpoint-03-{tests,build}.txt`. ## Checkpoint 04: generation repairs and responsive exports

- Added real 1×1, 1×2, 1×3 and 1×4 plates (3024, 3023, 3623, 3710). The one-plate remainder in a narrow clock detail now packs without enlarging that detail. Catalog dimensions, four rotations, exact stock, BOM/source allocation, web/SVG/PDF, USDZ, LDraw and saved Photon handoffs share the same geometry. Part 3023 exports as the canonical LDraw `3023b.dat`; see `part-catalog-increment.md` for official sources. Existing saved designs remain unchanged.
- The recorded Bell Tower plan exposed a packing-order failure: 488 pieces left one plate unsupported, although the same colored cells can form a valid 487-piece assembly. Generation now tries one alternate traversal only after support/connectivity failure, and accepts it only when the entire geometry check passes. Exact stock, explicit budgets and the original shape still apply. Valid first packings are retained. This is a bounded heuristic improvement, not an exhaustive packing solver.
- Streaming failures retain the checker feedback through the API and browser. The UI shows a short explanation with expandable exact attempt details and preserves the prompt/current design. The browser also stops a stalled connection after 65 seconds, allowing five seconds beyond the server's 60-second planning deadline. A localhost integration test reproduced Vite keeping a stream open after an upstream disconnect; the proxy now closes it promptly while retaining normal complete responses. The exact cause of the development API restart was not established. The dev API reloads on source changes.
- V2 boxes can omit local cutout volumes for hollow interiors, doors and windows. Cuts affect only their parent box and remain local in translated repeats; they never erase other boxes or trim slope parts. Independent positive wall segments and a compact cutout plan produce exactly the same 1,177 colored occupied cells. The 78-piece furnished-room fixture passed geometry/admission checks and its rendered interior, windows and doorway were inspected. This establishes compiler behavior, not a guarantee of AI request fidelity.
- PDF diagrams render in a worker, one manual at a time. Concurrent requests for the same design/inventory snapshot share work; queued inputs are frozen. Regression checks cover the main event loop, failed-job recovery and edits made while a job waits. The castle's 22,847,414-byte PDF took 21.22 seconds while 21 local status requests completed (median 0.95 ms, maximum 2.02 ms). Rendering itself is still expensive. Run `npm run check:large-export` against the local API to reproduce without any AI request.
- The actual browser showed the disabled, labeled manual-progress control, allowed closing the export dialog and opening Pieces during preparation, and downloaded a 22,853,127-byte PDF to Downloads. The saved revision ID accounts for the size difference from the curated sample export. The UI reports completion and exposes export errors. Worker-rendered castle PDF text and representative page renders matched the preceding renderer.
- Evidence: `tests/fixtures/hollow-room-plan.ts`, `output/cutouts-local/room.png`, `output/complex-local/background-export.json`, `output/complex-local/manual-pending.jpg`, and `output/checkpoint-04-{tests,build}.txt`.
- The owned-inventory heuristic can reject a feasible exact-stock packing: recompiling the hollow-room fixture with precisely its earlier BOM fails despite that known arrangement. A corner-lookahead preference changes an earlier part choice and spends a later-needed part. The same failure occurs without cutouts. Inventory is never overspent; general packing completeness remains unresolved.

## Live Gemini results

- Checkpoint 03 latency changes: replaced three sequential 90-second calls with at most two attempts sharing a 60-second provider deadline, explicit SDK single-attempt behavior, and streamed planning/checking/repair progress. `gemini-3.8-flash` uses its supported LOW thinking level; the [official model documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash) describes MEDIUM as its default. A successful LOW-versus-MEDIUM comparison has **not** been measured.
- First new live request returned provider 504 after **59.209 seconds** (59.226 seconds total), before producing a plan. A subsequent complex observatory request returned **429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED** after **184 ms** (198 ms total). Google reported the project's daily free-tier limit of **20 requests** for this model and a retry delay of 16,908 seconds at that moment. The app now reports quota exhaustion separately and does not retry that provider failure. Raw redacted reports are `output/latency/gemini-3.8-flash-low.json` and `output/complex-live/gemini-3.8-flash-low.json`. No billing, model or project settings were changed. This was the checkpoint-03 status; the user subsequently funded the account, and the later results below supersede that quota blocker.

After the user added $10 to the same project, the existing key returned HTTP 200 from Gemini. Three detailed-observatory requests (six provider calls including repairs) took 30.89, 35.17, and 36.09 seconds. The first two were rejected for untileable thin sections, unsupported floors/pillars, and a disconnected single-course foundation. Support guidance now explains the four-stud part span, actual stud/plate dimensions, interior piers, tileable thin features, and connected foundations; repair feedback includes part IDs and coordinates.

The third request produced **520 pieces**, including 20 real slopes, in **36.09 seconds with one repair**, ID `d5569b7c-e9bc-49d5-8e7d-ee16e8b2be0c`. Its geometry, BOM and earlier-support ordering pass. Reviewed opposite browser elevations, a first-floor cutaway, and step 27 piece 1 → 2. It exports 52 steps (at most 12 pieces each), a 55-page / 5.67 MB PDF in 4.21 seconds, and a valid USDZ archive. **Request fidelity is partial:** the white eaves use full bricks rather than plates, the roof is only partially sloped, and the stairs do not provide the requested terrace access; full internal circulation is absent. Do not describe it as a faithful architectural or physically tested model. The committed `tests/fixtures/live-observatory-plan.json` reproduces the successful plan offline. The exact user prompt **“the Morehead Bell Tower at UNC Chapel Hill”** produced a 363-piece / 37-step model in 18.69 seconds using the updated source, after one repair. An isolated regression captured that failure; the new narrow-plate tests now require preserving the original four-plate height rather than inflating it. Unlimited-mode errors now identify unsupported part geometry instead of incorrectly blaming the user's inventory. This led to the narrow-plate catalog fix, rather than claiming the successful individual run established reliability.
